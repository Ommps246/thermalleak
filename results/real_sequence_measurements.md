# Measurements on a real static-camera thermal sequence

First numbers in this project produced from real thermal imagery rather than the
simulator. Two results: the detector's **false alarm rate on real static
scenes**, and a re-measurement of the **two injection rungs** on a real
substrate, replacing figures previously taken on a simulator no-leak pair.

## Data and the gain check

**OTCBVS Benchmark Dataset 03** (OSU Color-Thermal Database), thermal sequences
`1a`, `2a`, `3a`, from <http://vcipl-okstate.org/pbvs/bench/>. 320x240, 8-bit,
static camera, outdoor pedestrian scene.

Before anything else: **do the frames share a fixed gain, or is each one
auto-scaled?** Per-frame auto-scaling would make inter-frame differences
meaningless, since the mapping would move with scene content. Every frame does
hit exactly 0 and exactly 255, which looks like a per-frame min/max stretch --
but it is not:

| check | result | reading |
|---|---|---|
| pixels at 0 | 12.6% of frame | whole clipped cold regions, not the one pixel a stretch pins |
| pixels at 255 | 0.06% of frame | genuine hot saturation |
| static background level, 1054 frames | 37.44-38.41 DN, sd 0.14 DN | fixed mapping |
| corr(hot content, background level) | -0.05 (1a), +0.34 (2a) | AGC would drive this strongly negative |
| per-pixel temporal noise, static mask | 0.29-0.31 DN | |

**Fixed gain, confirmed.** The rails are real saturation of a fixed mapping.

## Frame selection

The sequences are busy -- no frame is ever globally empty, and the largest
never-disturbed rectangle is a 320x16 strip of sky. So the measurements use a
sub-region with no pedestrian traffic instead: sequence `3a`, **128x128 crop at
rows 48:176, cols 0:128**, restricted to the **349 of 1700 frames** in which
nothing inside that crop deviates by more than 12 DN from the sequence's
temporal median. Those 349 frames span 106 s in 40 separate runs, so pairs can
be separated by up to ~95 s. Background texture in the crop: 3.33 K sd.

DN are converted to kelvin with a **fixed** linear map, `20-45 C across 0-255`
(98 mK/DN), identical for every frame. This matters: `load_as_field` normalises
by each frame's own min/max, which for independently-cropped frames would
renormalise each one and erase precisely the inter-frame drift being measured.

## 1. False alarm rate on real static scenes

400 pairs of quiet frames, separations 1-95 s, **no scene change and no injected
leak**. How often does the detector fire?

| | fires |
|---|---|
| physics-informed | **35.2%** (141/400) |
| differencing only | **49.5%** (198/400) |
| *for reference: simulated FPR at the tuned operating point* | *5.3%* |

By separation:

| separation | physics | diff-only | n |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1-5 s | 13.1% | 34.4% | 61 |
| 5-15 s | 17.5% | 47.4% | 57 |
| 15-30 s | 58.9% | 73.3% | 90 |
| 30-60 s | 33.3% | 43.2% | 132 |
| 60-110 s | 43.3% | 45.0% | 60 |

Two readings, pointing opposite ways.

**The physics stage does real work on real data.** It removes about a third of
the false alarms plain differencing raises, 50% -> 35%. This is the first
evidence for the project's central claim that was not generated by the simulator
that was built to demonstrate it, and it is the single most useful sentence
here.

**The simulator is materially optimistic about nuisances.** 35% is roughly seven
times the 5.3% the simulated evaluation reports, so an operating point chosen on
simulated data will fire far more often in the field than its nominal setting
promises. Any deployment threshold must be set on real data.

Robustness: the rate is 34.5% selecting frames at a 12 DN quiet threshold and
32.0% at 8 DN, so it is not an artefact of loose selection. No frame in the
sequence is ever quiet below 5 DN (0.5 K) somewhere in the crop -- there is
always some real change. The firing candidates have a median peak of 0.90 K
against a median frame-to-frame maximum difference of 1.18 K, so the detector is
responding to genuine ~1 K scene excursions, not to nothing.

Caveat on transfer: this is an outdoor scene with sun and wind, considerably more
dynamic than a laptop on a bench. Read 35% as an **upper bound** for the
controlled indoor capture `PROTOCOL_real_data.md` describes.

### Tested and rejected: was 35% inflated by a mismatched thermal length?

The frames above were scored with `abs_casing` at 2.5 mm/px -- the injection
study's device-scale convention, which for an outdoor scene at real standoff is
a fiction. The hypothesis was that the length test, keyed to a thermal length
unrelated to the scene, was manufacturing false alarms, and that scene-
appropriate parameters would cut the rate.

Scale was measured from the data rather than assumed: 2527 pedestrian blobs,
height against image row to handle perspective, a standing adult taken as 1.70 m.
The gradient is clean (121 mm/px at the top of the frame to 46 mm/px at the
bottom), and at the crop the scale is **77.3 mm/px** -- the 128 px crop spans
9.9 m. `concrete` and `asphalt` were added to `MATERIALS` with an active layer
of 0.15 m and convection halved to keep the model's two-face `2h` correct for
ground that loses heat from one face.

**The hypothesis is wrong.**

| config | L (px) | FAR physics | FAR diff-only | reduction | 50% limit | peak K at limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| abs_casing @ 2.5 mm/px | 4.54 | **35.2%** | 49.5% | 29% | 0.0133 W | 0.61 |
| concrete @ 77.3 mm/px | 1.53 | **37.8%** | 70.8% | 47% | 3.16 W | 0.80 |
| asphalt @ 77.3 mm/px | 1.12 | **35.0%** | 70.8% | 51% | 17.8 W | 5.45 |

The physics false alarm rate does not move: 35.2% -> 37.8% / 35.0%. Concrete is
the fair comparison, because its sensitivity is within 30% of the original in
temperature terms (0.80 K against 0.61 K peak rise at the 50% limit) -- so the
detector is not merely being switched off, it is operating at comparable
sensitivity and still firing on a third of static pairs. Asphalt's matching 35%
is not evidence of anything: it comes with a 5.45 K sensitivity, nine times
worse, which is the degenerate case the concrete row rules out.

Sensitivity in *watts* differs by two orders of magnitude across the rows and
should be ignored; it is the `k*d` conversion, not detector behaviour. Peak
temperature rise is the scale-free comparison and it barely moves.

**So 35% is close to what real data actually costs, and the original number
stands.** Two things follow. The thermal length was not the problem -- the real
causes are catalogued in `real_sequence/false_alarm_taxonomy.md`, and 41% of
them turn out to be a border artefact in `register.py` rather than anything
physical. And the physics stage's *relative* value is larger at scene-
appropriate settings, not smaller: it removes 47% of differencing's false alarms
with concrete against 29% with the device-scale convention, because
differencing-only degrades badly (49.5% -> 70.8%) when the matched filter
narrows.

## 2. The two injection rungs, re-measured on real frames

12 base pairs (separations 22-93 s) x 3 draws per power, 13 powers from 1 mW to
1 W. Identical frames and identical injected leaks in both arms; the only
difference is which frame the detector is handed as its reference.

| rung | 50% limit | previously (simulator pair) |
|---|---|---|
| injection, shared background | **0.0100 W** | 0.0032 W |
| injection, two frames of the sequence | **0.0178 W** | 0.0100 W |
| gap | **1.8x** | 3.2x |

Both rungs got harder on real data, which is the expected direction. The *gap*
narrowed, because the shared-background arm got harder too -- real texture is
worse clutter than a simulated frame, so the shortcut has less left to flatter.
Both figures now sit on the same real substrate, which is what makes the 1.8x
between them meaningful; the previous pair mixed a simulator background with a
simulator "sequence".

## What these numbers are not

**8-bit, so: real texture and real nuisances, not real radiometry.** There is no
temperature in these files. The watt figures rest on the declared 20-45 C span
across the 8-bit range; scale that assumption and the limits scale with it. The
assumption-free content is in DN and in the *relative* comparison between arms,
which shares the assumption and therefore cancels it.

**Not this scene's true spatial scale.** Frames are treated as device-scale at
2.5 mm/px, the injection study's standing convention, so the material thermal
length of 4.5 px is a fiction for an outdoor scene at ~10 m. The clutter is real;
the geometry is a stand-in.

**Not a real leak.** The injected source obeys the same K0 physics the detector
tests for. Only the capture protocol closes that loop.

## Reproducing

    curl -O http://vcipl-okstate.org/pbvs/bench/Data/03/3a.zip && unzip 3a.zip

Crop rows 48:176, cols 0:128; keep frames whose crop deviates <12 DN from the
sequence temporal median; convert with kelvin = 293.15 + DN*25/255; save .npy.
Then:

    python3 scripts/run_injection.py --baseline <early>.npy --background <late>.npy \
        --sweep --compare --repeats 8 --mpp 0.0025
